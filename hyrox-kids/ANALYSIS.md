## What it is

Not a tutorial, and not a talking-head explainer. It is a
**music-driven, chaptered, day-in-the-life montage** following one
child through a whole race day, start to finish. No voiceover, no
interview to camera, no captions except short chapter titles in
plain white capitals, centred on screen.

That structure is the whole trick: the viewer does not need to know
HYROX. They follow a kid they can root for, through a clear sequence,
and feel the finish.

## Visual language

- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920, 25 fps, cinematic grade —
  dark blacks, warm skin, saturated purple / neon-green / yellow.
- **Brand colours:** purple (Youngstars arch, check-out pillars),
  black (kit, barriers, floor), neon green (shoes, kit stripe,
  finisher patch), yellow (finish arch). White chapter text only.
- **Camera:** handheld / tracking, mostly at the child's eye level
  or low, following from behind or side-on. Lots of close-ups that
  prove effort without showing strain: shoes, hands on the SkiErg,
  wristband, patch, sign.
- **Cutting:** ~1–2 seconds per shot, 50+ shots in 58 seconds.
  No shot lingers. The chapter label holds for 2–4 seconds while
  2–3 shots play under it.
- **Sound:** music-driven (no speech is captioned, and no voiceover
  is needed to follow it). The edit is cut to the beat — arrivals
  and the finish land on musical lifts. Audio was extracted locally
  (`audio.wav`) but not transcribed, because there is no spoken
  narration to transcribe.
- **People:** family is present throughout — mother at registration,
  at the barrier cheering, at the finish hug. That is the emotional
  spine: it is a family day, not a solo athlete film.
- **No hard sell:** no price, no "sign up" card, no URL on screen.
  The CTA is the feeling + the account name + hashtags. The
  collectable patch ("ADD TO THE COLLECTION") is the retention hook:
  come back and collect the next one.

## Why it works for promoting HYROX for kids

1. **One child, one day, one arc.** Arrival → preparation → effort →
   finish → reward → home. A parent can picture their own child in it.
2. **Effort looks fun, not punishing.** Stations are short, varied,
   and shown in quick succession. The child is tired at the finish,
   then immediately hugged and proud.
3. **Parents are the audience, kids are the hero.** The film is shot
   for a parent scrolling Instagram: safe, joyful, communal, and
   clearly supervised (staff in hi-vis, barriers, check-out).
4. **Collectability.** The patch is a small, physical, repeatable
   reason to return. It is shown in close-up, not just mentioned.
5. **Brand is in the environment, not in a logo sting.** Purple
   arches, black barriers with "YOUNG STARS", kit — the venue *is*
   the advert.

## What is NOT in the video

- No training plan, distances, weights, ages per category, or
  timings. A parent who wants to enter still has to click through
  to HYROX.
- No other children's names, no results / times on screen.
- No voiceover explaining HYROX rules. If you do not know HYROX,
  you learn "kids run + do stations + finish + get a patch", which
  is enough for a promo.
- No price, date of the next event, or location beyond Birmingham
  in the video itself (that is in the caption / hashtags).

## Reverse-engineering summary

The "technology" here is not AI editing — it is a **shot-list +
chapter edit**:

```
Hook (arch, 0–3s) → Registration → Sign prep → Warm-up →
Race stations in order → Hydration (breather) → Back into it →
Finish (emotional peak) → Check-out → Patch (reward) → Home time
```

Each chapter = 1 white title + 2–4 shots + 1 close-up detail.
Render that template with your own child / event, your own
purple/green/yellow brand, and the same music-driven 1–2s cutting,
and you have the same film. See `REPRODUCTION.md` in this folder
for the shot list, edit recipe, and safeguarding checklist.
